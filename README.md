# TIME Protocol

**A human-time stablecoin protocol backed by verified work and real payments.**

TIME Protocol proposes a new monetary primitive: a stable asset anchored not to a fiat currency, but to verified human time.

Today’s dominant stablecoins are mostly U.S. dollar-pegged tokens. They are useful payment instruments, but the term “stablecoin” is misleading. They are stable only relative to the dollar. For anyone whose economic life is denominated in euros, pesos, naira, yen, reais, rupees, or other currencies, dollar stablecoins fluctuate constantly. They are not universally stable; they are tokenized dollar exposure.

TIME Protocol offers a different reference unit:

> **One TIME represents one verified hour of human productive capacity, minted only when real work is contracted, verified, and paid for.**

TIME is not a better dollar stablecoin. It is a different category: a **human-time stablecoin**.

---

## Core Thesis

**USD stablecoins are stable against a currency. TIME is stable against civilization’s primary scarce asset: human life-hours.**

A dollar is a political unit.  
An hour is a biological unit.

USD stablecoins inherit the volatility, inflation, monetary policy, geopolitical power, and banking dependencies of the U.S. dollar system. TIME is anchored in something more universal: the finite daily time of verified human beings.

---

## Why USD Stablecoins Are Misnamed

USD stablecoins stabilize one exchange rate: token-to-dollar.

They do not stabilize:

- purchasing power;
- local-currency value;
- labor value;
- housing affordability;
- food, energy, education, or healthcare costs;
- global wage parity;
- human economic opportunity.

A USDC or USDT holder owns a token designed to remain redeemable for one U.S. dollar. But the dollar itself moves against every other currency and against the real economy.

The more precise term for most USD stablecoins is:

> **Tokenized offshore dollar liquidity.**

They are powerful. But they are not universally stable.

---

## The TIME Alternative

TIME Protocol stabilizes value against verified human time.

Instead of asking, “How many dollars is this worth?” TIME asks:

> **How many verified human hours does this represent?**

That shift changes the foundation of digital money.

Bitcoin introduced machine proof-of-work.  
TIME introduces human proof-of-work.

Bitcoin converts energy into scarcity.  
TIME converts verified human contribution into programmable money.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Human-Time Unit of Account

One TIME represents one verified hour of human productive capacity.

This does not mean one TIME must always equal a fixed dollar amount. Fiat exchange rates may float. The stability claim is different:

> **1 TIME = 1 verified human hour.**

The hour, not the dollar, is the reference unit.

### 2. Work-Backed Issuance

TIME is not minted merely because someone exists or claims time.

TIME is minted when:

1. a verified human accepts a work contract;
2. the relevant time is reserved and cannot be double-booked;
3. work is completed or otherwise contractually satisfied;
4. payment is received from a real counterparty;
5. the protocol records proof of humanity, proof of time, proof of work, and proof of payment.

The backing is not abstract labor. It is **market-clearing payment for verified human work**.

### 3. Biological Scarcity

Each verified human has only 24 hours per day.

No central bank can print more hours.  
No government can inflate the length of a day.  
No corporation can issue synthetic human life.

TIME supply is bounded by:

> **verified humans × verified hours × real paid demand**

### 4. Non-Sovereign Monetary Anchor

USD stablecoins extend the U.S. dollar system on-chain.

TIME offers a non-sovereign human reference unit. It can be used globally without making the U.S. dollar the default monetary denominator for every worker, DAO, community, application, or government.

### 5. Programmable Wage Economy

USD stablecoins tokenize cash.

TIME tokenizes the wage economy.

TIME can become infrastructure for:

- work contracts;
- payroll;
- freelancing;
- creator labor;
- volunteer hours;
- UBI;
- taxation;
- escrow;
- proof-of-service;
- credentials;
- scheduling;
- reputation;
- human capital coordination;
- AI-era labor protection.
